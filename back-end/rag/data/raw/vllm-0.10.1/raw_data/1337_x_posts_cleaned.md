## 2. The Piscine and admission-related announcements

### What the Piscine is described as

The posts describe the Piscine as an intensive, approximately four-week challenge involving coding, problem-solving, collaboration, and learning under pressure. Candidates are encouraged to learn by doing, ask peers for help, experiment, debug, and try again. The posts emphasize persistence and peer learning rather than receiving step-by-step instruction.

Posts refer to “Pre-Entry Day” before the Piscine, and to results being made available through candidates' personal application profiles.

### Selection and results

The supplied posts mention that:

- Candidates should check their personal application space or profile for their selection results and Piscine details.
- Selection and available places are communicated through the candidate platform.
- Candidates who are not selected are encouraged to continue learning, but the posts do not promise automatic admission to a later Piscine.
- One announcement says that a randomized selection process was introduced in 2026 to replace a first-come, first-served process. This is a claim from that announcement and should be checked against current official admissions guidance.

### Historical 2026 dates — do not present as upcoming dates

The posts explicitly gave the following dates:

- First Piscine in 2026: **13 July 2026**.
- Second Piscine in 2026: **10 August 2026**.
- A separate announcement asked candidates who had successfully completed a Piscine in 2026 or an earlier year to confirm entry for the **2026/2027 academic year**.
- Registration/entry confirmation period in that announcement: **23–28 September 2026**.
- School start date stated in that announcement: **12 October 2026**.

These dates are historical as of 24 October 2026. They must not be used to answer “When is the next Piscine?” or “When does school start next year?” The posts do not provide future dates.

Another post says that the first batch of “Check-in” slots had opened and that additional places would be released later. It tells candidates to log in to their personal candidate profile to reserve an available slot. No reliable date or current availability is supplied.

## 3. Learning approach and student experience

Across the posts, 1337 describes its learning approach using the following themes:

- **Learning by doing:** students work on challenges and projects rather than only consuming theoretical material.
- **Peer learning:** students explain concepts, exchange ideas, collaborate, and help one another.
- **Autonomy:** students are expected to investigate problems and find solutions independently.
- **Iteration:** debugging, testing, failing, adapting, and retrying are described as normal parts of learning.
- **Problem-solving:** students are encouraged to break down unfamiliar problems and build working solutions.
- **Community:** teamwork, shared experiences, and mutual support are presented as important parts of student life.

One post describes the experience as having “no lectures” and “no step-by-step instructions.” Treat this as the school's promotional description of its pedagogy, not proof that every learning activity at every campus has no guidance or structured support.

### Peer support during the Piscine

A post describes a student named Firdaws bringing Pisciners together to explain difficult concepts during the final week of a Piscine. Other posts mention candidates sharing advice and encouragement anonymously with Pisciners approaching the end of the experience. These examples illustrate peer support; they do not establish a formal tutoring service.
